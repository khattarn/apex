Channel Strategy — US / Canada / Australia

Which channels to lead with, how the funnel works, and how each market responds. Grounded in current (2025–26) benchmarks.
PracticeDesk Email-led Issued: 2026-06-28

1 · The headline finding

Apex was built WhatsApp-first — correct for India, wrong for these three markets.

WhatsApp is a minority channel in the West: about 23% of US internet users, ~33% in Australia (its best of the three), and lower again in Canada. Email, by contrast, is the channel B2B buyers prefer — 73–77% name it their top outbound channel, and buyers typically engage across ~2.5 channels (email + a LinkedIn touch + one well-timed call).

Correction for Apex: in US/CA/AU, WhatsApp is not the cold tip of the spear. It is the closer — used after a lead opts in. Email is the engine; voice is the high-intent follow-up.

3 · Why each channel — the data

Email — primary

Cheapest, most scalable, the preferred B2B channel. Cold reply rates average 3–5%; top performers reach 15–25% via better hooks, tight targeting and small batches (<100/campaign replies best at ~5.5%). Timeline hooks beat problem-based hooks 2.3×; Thursday + evening (8–11pm) reply best. Open rate is now unreliable (Apple Mail preloads pixels) — optimise for replies.

Phone / Aria voice — secondary, high-intent

B2B cold-call success ~2.3–6.7%; 57% of senior buyers prefer phone; ~8 attempts to reach someone; 4–5pm converts best. Business numbers are largely exempt from Do-Not-Call rules in all three countries, so calling businesses is legal. Best used as a follow-up to email engagement, not cold-first at scale.

Social — air cover, not a lead engine

LinkedIn is a useful second touch (DM after email) for professional verticals (dentist, accountant, lawyer). Instagram/Facebook are brand/inbound — best for salons. Outbound social does not reliably book demos for local-service SMBs; it warms, the funnel converts.

5 · What changes in Apex

  • Re-rank channels: email = engine (fixed), Aria voice = engagement-triggered follow-up, WhatsApp = post-opt-in. The scheduler currently treats WA broadcast as a primary cold push for these markets — effort spent on a minority channel. (Handoff Change #6.)
  • Separate Canada track: CASL makes "180 emails/day, all markets" a real legal risk. Canada gets lower volume, published-business-emails only, flawless unsubscribe, EBR/implied-consent windows respected. (Handoff Change #6.)
  • Hook + timing test: A/B timeline-style hooks against the current moment-based hooks, and send Thursday evening (local). Let Signal decide the winner — don't assume.
Before scaling sends in any market: move cold outreach to a subdomain (e.g. go.practicedesk.live), set SPF/DKIM/DMARC, warm up, and ramp slowly — broken or wrong-vertical sends from the main domain damage deliverability for everything.
