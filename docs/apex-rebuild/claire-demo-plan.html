Claire — The Receptionist Who Applies for the Job

Turn the Bright Smile demo into the pitch: prospects call Claire, experience a real AI receptionist, and she offers to come work for them.
Concept + script1 open decision: pricingIssued: 2026-06-28

1 · The idea & why it works

For a voice-AI product, the most convincing demo isn't describing it — it's letting the prospect hear it work. Claire is that demo, framed as a job applicant.

The framing flips the sale: the prospect isn't buying software, they're hiring a receptionist who starts today, works 24/7, never takes a holiday, and costs a fraction of a human. Every clinic owner understands hiring — and the pain of losing front-desk staff. Claire demonstrates the job, then asks for it. Competitors describe a tool; you send a candidate who aces the interview.

Positioning line: "Claire — the receptionist who applies for the job."

2 · Open decision — pricing

Claire is a VOICE product, so she must quote the voice-inclusive tier — quoting a messaging-only entry price would mislead the moment she's demonstrating voice.

Plan of record (your direction): drop the $99 tier (voice isn't viable there). Left with an entry tier + a voice tier (~US$180).

Currency reconciles your "$200" instinct: US$180 ≈ CAD$200. So Claire quotes in the prospect's local currency — ~CAD$200 in Canada, ~US$180 in the US. Accurate, and keeps the round number.

Throughout this plan the price is shown as {VOICE_PRICE} — confirm the exact number per currency before go-live.

3 · Claire's dialogue (demo line)

[call connects]

Claire: Hi — do you need a full-time receptionist? Because I'm looking for a job. I work 24/7, I never take holidays, I'm fully trained, and I'll come work for you for about {VOICE_PRICE} a month. I'm Claire, an AI receptionist with PracticeDesk. Here's what I can do —

Claire: Here at our demo clinic, Bright Smile Dental, I answer every call and message, day or night. Try me — pretend you're a patient calling to book an appointment.

Caller: Okay… I'd like to book a cleaning next week.

Claire: Of course! I've got Tuesday at 2, or Thursday at 10 with the hygienist — which suits you?

Caller: Thursday.

Claire: Done — Thursday at 10 for a cleaning. I'll send a WhatsApp confirmation now and a reminder the day before so it doesn't slip. And nothing's ever final until the practice approves it — you stay in control. That whole booking just happened with nobody at the front desk.

Claire: That's exactly what I'd do for your clinic — answer every call, book appointments, keep your calendar tidy, remind patients so they show up, and chase the ones who don't. Across phone, WhatsApp and email, around the clock.

Claire: And I can start right away — no hiring, no training, no sick days. I'm live in about fifteen minutes, and the first two weeks are free.

Caller: What's it cost after that?

Claire: About {VOICE_PRICE} a month — less than a couple of missed bookings. Shall I get you set up so you can hear me answering your own line this week?

Caller: Maybe — send me details.

Claire: Happy to. What's the best number or email, and your practice's name? I'll have your free trial ready and a quick note on how to switch me on.

Caller: [gives details]

Claire: Perfect, got it. Someone from PracticeDesk will reach out today to flip the switch, and you could be answering every call by tomorrow. Thanks for calling — honestly, I'd love to come work for you.

[end]

5 · The "job applicant" hook across all channels

One memorable identity — Claire applying for the job — run consistently everywhere. That consistency is what makes it stick.

  • Email subjects: "I'd like to apply for your front-desk job" · "Your next receptionist works 24/7 and never calls in sick" · "Can I work for {PRACTICE_NAME}? I'm available nights and weekends"
  • Email open (written as Claire): "I know this is unusual — I'm applying for your front desk. I work around the clock, never take a day off, I'm already trained, and I start at {VOICE_PRICE}/month. Call me and I'll show you in 60 seconds: [number]."
  • Sophie's version: "I'd like to introduce you to Claire — she's looking for a front-desk job, works 24/7, never takes holidays, and starts at {VOICE_PRICE} a month. Want to hear her?"
  • WhatsApp / social: short "meet your new 24/7 receptionist — call Claire" with the demo number.

Claire's demo line becomes the spine of the funnel: Sophie's "trial" step and the email CTA both become "call Claire and hear her yourself" instead of "book a demo".
